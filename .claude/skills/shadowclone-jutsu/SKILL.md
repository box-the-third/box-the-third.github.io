# Shadowclone Jutsu

In Naruto, the shadow clone jutsu splits one ninja into many, each doing different work, and everything every clone learns flows back to the original. That is exactly the problem this skill solves for people who feel scattered: they have worn many hats (jobs, side hustles, family duties, community roles) and have never seen those versions of themselves side by side. Once they do, the pattern, the strengths and the next move become obvious.

The deliverable is one **master document**: the Original at the top, then one Clone per role, each with named superpowers grounded in what the person actually did.

## Why it works this way

People with "no idea" rarely lack ability. They lack a vocabulary for what they already do. A clone gives each role a name and a power set, which turns "I just helped out at my uncle's shop" into "The Closer: reads a customer in ten seconds." So your job is translation: from lived work to named, evidence-backed abilities. Never invent abilities, numbers or titles the person did not give you. A power with no proof is a costume; a power with proof is a skill they can put on a CV or say in an interview.

## Workflow

### 1. Draw out the roles (interview)

Talk like a good friend, not a form. Ask in two or three short rounds, not twenty questions at once.

- **Round 1, the roster.** "List every role you've played: paid jobs, internships, side projects, things you got roped into, roles at home or in your community." Prompt for the forgotten ones: the person everyone asks for help with X, the class rep, the one who runs the group chat. If they say "I've done nothing," ask what people thank them for.
- **Round 2, for each role:** what did you actually have to do (the verbs), what did you get good at, what's one result or proof (a number, a thank-you, something that shipped), what drained you.
- **Round 3, direction.** What do you want next, what feels like you, and what would you drop?

If the person pastes a CV, LinkedIn text or notes, mine it first and only ask about the gaps. If they have given a lot already, skip rounds they have answered. Keep the pace brisk: the goal is momentum, not a perfect intake.

### 2. Build each Clone

Use this card for every role. Keep each field short and concrete.

- **Codename**: a short, memorable title (The Mentor, The Engine). Make it feel like theirs, not generic.
- **The role in real life**: job title, where, when.
- **Origin: what I had to do**: 2 to 3 lines on the actual work, in verbs.
- **Superpowers (3)**: each power has a name, a plain-English meaning, and **proof** from their real experience. Format: *Power Name: what it lets them do. Proof: ...* Powers should be transferable skills phrased as abilities ("Turns a messy room of strangers into a team") rather than job duties.
- **Weakness / chakra cost**: what this clone drains or does badly. Honest, useful for choosing roles.
- **Summon when**: 2 or 3 situations where this clone is the right one to call.
- **Next mission**: one concrete action this week that levels the clone up.

Where a number or outcome is missing, write "Proof needed" and give a one-line way to get it (for example, ask a past manager for a testimonial) instead of making something up.

### 3. Assemble the master document

Structure, in this order:

1. **The Original**: one paragraph naming the thread that runs through every clone (what they are really about), plus a one-line "superpower stack".
2. **Clone Roster**: a table with codename, role, top power, energy level (drains / neutral / charges).
3. **One section per Clone**: the card above.
4. **Combo Jutsu**: 2 to 4 pairings of clones that create something none could alone (for example, a teacher clone plus a content clone yields a course business). This is where the "make the most of yourself" insight lives.
5. **Missing Clones**: roles they have never held but their powers point to, with the smallest first step toward each.
6. **30-Day Training Plan**: one small mission per week, drawn from the clone cards.

For depth and tone, read `references/example-nahiyan.md`. It is a complete worked example built from the skill author's own roles; use it as a quality bar and for archetype inspiration, never copy its clones or facts into someone else's document.

### 4. Deliver

Produce the master document as a shareable, editable doc using whatever docs capability is available (a Claude Docs connector or docs skill, a Word file, or a markdown file as the fallback). Tell the person where it is. Then offer one refinement pass: "Which clone feels least like you, and which feels most?" and adjust.

## Voice and guardrails

- Playful, warm, a little anime, never cringe. A Naruto reference per section is plenty; clarity beats cleverness.
- Plain language. If a power name needs explaining, the name is wrong.
- Honesty over hype. Weaknesses and "Proof needed" are features, not failures; they make the document trustworthy and actionable.
- Treat the person's information as theirs. Do not add employers, dates, metrics or credentials they did not state.
- If someone has only one or two roles, build those clones deeply and lean on Missing Clones; do not pad with fake roles.
